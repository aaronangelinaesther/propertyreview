# Savills Auction — 29 & 30 September 2026 (Catalogue #243) — Property Review

Source: Savills online auction catalogue — https://auctions.savills.co.uk/auctions/29--30-september-2026-243. A much larger UK-wide sale than the immediately preceding one: **352 lots total** — residential lots mainly on **Tuesday 29 September** (remote bidding, 9:00am-style session), a **commercial section of ~95 lots (231–325) on Wednesday 30 September**, plus a further batch of residential lots (326–398, also mostly Wednesday 30 September — a handful of individual lots' exact day could not be confirmed from the catalogue text; these are flagged in the relevant file).

**Scope:** the same filter as the two earlier reviews in this repo — residential lots (houses/flats; garages, land-only, ground-rent-only and commercial lots excluded) in **Greater London, Greater Manchester, and the City of Birmingham**. Originally **93 lots** — 85 London, 7 Greater Manchester, 1 Birmingham; **now 99 lots** after a 23 September re-check added 6 new lots the catalogue gained after initial research (see below) — roughly **35% more than the previous Savills catalogue's 73 lots**, reflecting this sale's much bigger overall size (383 lots vs. 188).

Every lot has: an area rating (growth potential, guide-vs-comparable value, social status/desirability, proximity to shops, proximity to schools), a **rental estimate and gross yield** (annual rent ÷ guide price, plus a fair-value yield against the researched local comparable), and per-lot risk flags.

## Cross-cutting finding: an unusually large number of re-offered lots

The single most important pattern across this review is how many lots are **exact re-offers of unsold lots from the immediately preceding Savills catalogue (15–16 September 2026, #242), two weeks earlier, at the same address and often the same guide price.** This is a much higher rate than would be expected by chance, and each research batch flagged it independently:

- **Lot 20, Hayes UB4 (£300,000)** = old catalogue's **Lot 37**, the *second-ranked top pick overall* in the previous review (confirmed freehold 4-bed, Crossrail-adjacent, Old Vinyl Factory regeneration). No obvious defect was found to explain the failed sale — worth a fresh look, but also a reminder that a strong desk-based case doesn't guarantee an auction-room result.
- **Lot 160, Nunhead SE15 (£325,000)** = old **Lot 71**, also a top pick last time (large 901 sq ft 2-bed at a steep discount) — the one unresolved flag both times is an **undisclosed lease term**, which looks like the real reason it didn't sell.
- **Lot 164, Altrincham** = old **Lot 30** (was £265,000) — re-offered initially with no guide price published at all; a guide of **£225,000** has since appeared (see the catalogue-update note above).
- **Lot 8, Westbourne Park W9** = old **Lot 41**; **Lot 72, Hanwell W7** = old catalogue's equivalent Hanwell lot; **Lot 145, Battersea (Plough Road)** = old **Lot 88**; **Lot 31, Stoke Newington (Leswin Road)** = old **Lot 130** (already flagged there as short-lease-likely); **Lot 337, Dalston** = old **Lot 170** (with zero resale transactions ever found for its building — possibly a resale-restricted scheme); **Lot 70, Leytonstone** also appears to be a re-offer; and **four South-East London lots (6, 139, 142, plus 160 above)** guide at identical prices to their #242 equivalents.

**Takeaway: unless a specific lot's failure to sell can be explained (e.g. Hayes, where no defect was found), treat an identical re-offer as a mild-to-moderate independent red flag on top of whatever the guide-to-value gap already suggests** — the market had a first look at the reserve and, at minimum, didn't clear it.

## Risk flags worth checking before bidding on anything

- **The re-offer cluster** — see "Cross-cutting finding" above. At minimum ten lots across this catalogue are identical re-listings of unsold #242 lots.
- **N16 (Stoke Newington/Shacklewell) short-lease cluster** — Lots 28, 31, 81, 351 (North London) all guide at 50–60% of comparable value with tenure unstated; Lot 31 is also a flagged re-offer.
- **Undisclosed HMO risk** — Lot 21, Boundaries Road, Balham SW12 (£850,000): a six-studio-room HMO where the studios may be unlawfully self-contained rather than shared-facility, layered on Wandsworth's Article 4 HMO restrictions — echoes the £1.4m Balham HMO lot flagged as high-risk in the previous review.
- **Undisclosed retirement/age-restricted tenure** — Lot 13, Newport Mews, Farnworth (£45,000): independent research found this is a 55+ sheltered-housing scheme (Places for People) allocated via the council system, **not stated in the Savills catalogue text** — potentially restricts subletting/resale.
- **Confirmed short lease** — Lot 86 (was Lot 165), Walthamstow E17: ground-floor flat has **46 years unexpired** (explicit in the catalogue, a genuine reversionary play, not a standard buy-to-let). *Lot number changed — see update note above.*
- **Distressed/receiver sales** — Lot 37 (Holland Park, £4.85m), Lot 69 (Fulham, confirmed £25,800/yr let), and Lot 138 (Abbey Wood) are all "By order of the Fixed Charge/LPA Receivers" — often explains an otherwise-puzzling discount, but confirm no onward complications.
- **Non-standard construction** — Lots 83 and 223, both in Hounslow TW4, are explicitly "non-standard construction," capping mortgageability to cash buyers despite the highest headline yields in West London (~10%+ on guide).
- **No published guide price ("TBA")** — Lot 77 (Camden) remains TBA; Lots 164 (Altrincham) and 339 (Hillingdon) have since had guides published (£225,000 and £200,000 respectively — see update note above).
- **Outlier/non-standard lot** — Lot 3, Sunray Farm, Carshalton SM5 (£850,000): a freehold smallholding (3-bed house + barns + ~26 acres of Green Belt grazing land) — a capital/lifestyle purchase, not a conventional yield play; Green Belt status limits development hope value.

## Method notes / limitations

- This catalogue's index pages fetched cleanly (no login needed) and, unlike the previous Savills review, the **listing descriptions themselves already state many key tenure facts directly** (new lease lengths, ground rent, confirmed passing rents, "non-standard construction," HMO certificates, receiver-sale status) — reducing (but not eliminating) the need for individual detail-page verification. No Savills-account detail pages were fetched for this review; all tenure/EPC notes beyond the catalogue's own text are either confirmed-by-catalogue or inferred from the guide-to-comparable-value gap.
- Rental estimates are **gross yields against the guide price** (a seller's minimum — auction lots typically sell above guide, so treat every guide-price yield as an upper bound), alongside a **fair-value yield** against each area's researched comparable sale price.
- Area and comparable-price research is from web search plus Zoopla/Rightmove/OnTheMarket/Land Registry postcode price pages, council/developer regeneration pages, and Ofsted, current to mid-September 2026. Two of the six research batches (South-East and South-West London) exhausted their web-search budget partway through and fell back on Rightmove postcode-page fetches plus general market knowledge for the remaining comparables — flagged inline in those files where it applies.
- The six batches were researched in parallel by separate passes and **no single consistent scoring formula was applied across all 99 lots** (93 original + 6 added on the 23 September re-check) — cross-batch "top picks" above are informal judgement calls, as in both earlier reviews.
- **Explicitly excluded** from scope: the commercial section (Lots 231–325); garages; land-only lots (including several with planning consent for a single dwelling, treated the same as pure land); ground-rent-only investments; and residential lots outside Greater London/Greater Manchester/City of Birmingham — e.g. Loughton IG10 (Essex, despite the IG postcode), Caterham CR3 and Guildford GU1 (Surrey), Tunbridge Wells (Kent), and Walsall/Darlaston/Wednesbury (West Midlands but not Birmingham). One large Grade II-listed Edgbaston building (Lot 99, £1,250,000) was also excluded as a non-standard "suitable for a variety of uses" development opportunity rather than a house/flat.
