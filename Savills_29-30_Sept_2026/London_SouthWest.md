A companion file, `London_SouthEast.md`, covers the SE-postcode half of South London separately. **Method:** individual Savills lot detail pages were not reachable this pass, so this batch is built from the catalogue index descriptions plus independent research — Rightmove house-price (sold-price) postcode pages, general market/rental knowledge, and area context. Where a Rightmove postcode page returned a **postcode-wide average across all property types/sizes**, a size-adjusted "fair value" for the specific unit type was estimated rather than using the raw average uncritically (flagged inline). Gross yield = annual rent ÷ **guide price** (an upper bound — auction lots typically sell above guide); a second **fair-value** yield is given against the researched comparable. Ratings 1 (weak) – 5 (strong).

## 7. Boundaries Road, Balham — SW12 8HF (Lot 21) — HIGH-RISK HMO LOT

### Area Rating

| Metric | Rating | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Growth potential | 4/5 | Same strong SW12 Balham fundamentals as Lots 17 and 38 — Northern line, established professional demand, currently in a price dip (SW12 sold prices 9% down YoY per Rightmove) that makes guides look comparatively attractive. |
| Avg. cost of comparable property | — | As a **single-family Victorian terrace reverted to normal use**, SW12 terraced houses average £1,199,867 postcode-wide; a period terrace on Boundaries Road needing modernisation would likely be worth **£1,000,000–£1,150,000** as a conventional house. As a **going-concern 6-studio HMO**, value depends entirely on the legality of that use (see risk section below) rather than on bricks-and-mortar comparables — **the £850,000 guide only makes sense once the HMO consent/licensing position is understood.** |
| Social status / desirability | 4/5 | Desirable Balham side street, comparable to the area's other lots. |
| Proximity to shops/amenities | 4/5 | Balham High Road and station within easy reach. |
| Proximity to schools | 4/5 | Wandsworth schools; largely irrelevant to an HMO tenant profile but relevant to the reversion-to-family-house case. |

**Verdict — read this before bidding.** This lot mirrors the single biggest risk lot in the *previous* Savills catalogue's South London review (Lot 12, 187 Balham High Road, £1.4m) almost exactly: a Victorian building marketed with **"six studio rooms"** and an explicit **HMO** label, needing modernisation. Two compounding risk layers apply:

1. **Licensing:** a property let to 5+ people from 2+ households is a **mandatory-licensable HMO** under the Housing Act 2004 across England; Wandsworth also operates additional/selective licensing schemes in parts of the borough. No licence is confirmed in the catalogue description — budget for the licensing process and any works a licence application demands (fire doors, means of escape, minimum room sizes).
2. **Planning:** the description of **"six studio rooms"** (rather than "six let bedrooms with shared kitchen/bathroom") suggests each unit may be self-contained — which, if so, means this is not simply a shared-facility HMO but a **subdivided building of up to six flats**, a materially bigger planning ask (individual space/fire/building-regs standards per unit, separate council tax banding, and full planning permission for the change of use/subdivision, not just an Article 4-affected permitted-development question). Wandsworth has a **borough-wide Article 4 Direction removing permitted development rights for C3-to-C4 (small HMO) conversions**, meaning full planning permission is required for HMO use regardless. **Whether six self-contained studios were ever lawfully created here is the single question that determines whether this lot is worth £850,000, more (as a licensed income asset) or considerably less (if the studios must be de-converted back to a single dwelling).** Get the planning history, any existing HMO licence, and building-regulations completion certificates before bidding — this is a planning/legal bet more than a straightforward property purchase, exactly like the prior catalogue's Balham HMO lot.
